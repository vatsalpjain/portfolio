Warm paper, quiet ink, and a little sage.

This is the second version of the portfolio design system. It keeps v1's book-like soul: one serif typeface, generous space and calm pages. It adds a voice of its own. Sage is the signature color, and it shows up in small, deliberate places: a dot after the name, a leaf between sections, a highlighter behind key words, a deep forest footer.

Your choices: warm and editorial mood · EB Garamond · sage accent · softly rounded corners (8–12px) · subtle transitions · light mode only · about, projects and writing sections · projects as a simple dated list.

Chapter one

Principles

  1. Calm over clever. If something draws attention to itself, it needs a reason. Content leads and the interface stays quiet.
  2. One voice. EB Garamond everywhere. Hierarchy comes from size, italics and space, never from extra fonts or heavy weights.
  3. Warm, not white. Pure white and pure black are banned. Everything sits on cream paper in soft brown-black ink.
  4. Sage is the signature. Sage marks everything clickable, and it's also the color of the site's "handwriting": the name dot, leaf dividers, drop caps, highlights, quote marks and the forest footer.
  5. Quietly playful. Small human touches, like a squiggle under a word, a breathing "currently" dot or a sidenote in the margin, give the site a voice without making it loud. Use one or two per screen, not all at once.
  6. Soft edges. Corners are 8–12px. Shadows are nearly invisible, and lines are hairlines.
  7. Slow and gentle. Transitions ease out over 160–700ms. Nothing bounces, and everything respects reduced-motion settings.
  8. Readable first. Lines are about 65 characters wide, the line height is 1.65, and all text meets WCAG AA contrast.

Chapter three

Typography

One family, EB Garamond, in regular, medium and italic. The scale is gentler than v1, with a smaller root (21px vs 23px) but more line height (1.65 vs ~1.5), so pages feel airier. Body text uses old-style figures, and dates use lining tabular figures so they align.

Chapter five

Motion

Motion here is the "smoother" part: everything changes gently instead of snapping. It uses one easing curve, a soft ease-out, at three speeds.

TokenValueUsed for
--dur-fast160mslink color, underline, tag hover, focus ring
--dur-base260msbuttons, row hover, nav underline slide, arrow nudge
--dur-slow700msscroll reveal: fade + 12px rise, once
--easecubic-bezier(.22,.61,.36,1)everything, no bounce or overshoot

With "reduce motion" turned on in the operating system, all transitions are disabled and reveals show immediately.
